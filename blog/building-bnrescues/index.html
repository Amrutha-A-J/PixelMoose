Building a home online for Brittany Niemi Rescues

How I built bnrescues.com for a Regina cat rescue run out of one woman’s pocket — and why the only bill she’ll ever get is for the domain.

Brittany Niemi runs a cat rescue in Regina with more than thirty cats in her care, and she pays for it out of her own pocket. Food, litter, vet bills, all of it. She deserves a lot more attention than she gets, so this first post on our blog is about her.

How I found her

I came across Brittany Niemi Rescues while I was looking to foster cats myself. Her available cats were listed on Facebook, scrolling past between everything else in people’s feeds. If you wanted to adopt or foster, you had to dig through posts and send messages to find out who was still looking for a home.

My husband and I drove to Regina to meet her and her kittens. We were surprised to find she rescues pigeons too. She takes in cats and kittens of every age and condition: bottle babies, seniors, and the ones who need extra care. She is completely overrun, and she still never says no to a cat in winter, because on the prairies in winter, no means death.

Why I had to help

I have been in her position. Back home in India, my husband and I took in cats, had them fixed, and either adopted them out or released them, in a country where a lot of people see cats as pests rather than pets. I know how much of yourself that takes, and how little time it leaves for anything else, like building a website.

Brittany had already bought her own domain, bnrescues.com. What she needed was a site that made it easy for adopters and fosters to find the right cat and reach her, without adding a single new bill.

What the site does

For adopters and fosters:

  • Every adoptable cat in one place, filterable by age group (kittens to seniors and special needs), sex and status, with search and sorting.
  • A page for each cat with a photo gallery, their story, personality tags, and whether they are good with kids and other cats.
  • Bonded pairs are marked, so cats who have to go home together do, and one application covers both.
  • Adoption and foster applications online. Adopters can apply for several cats at once; fosters can pick a cat or say they are open to any cat that needs them.
  • Light and dark mode, and it works well on a phone, which is where most people will find it.

And a private admin for the rescue, so Brittany never has to ask me to update anything:

  • Add and edit cats and upload their photos.
  • Review adoption and foster applications, and choose which inbox they go to.
  • Schedule meet & greets, with calendar invites that open in Outlook.
  • Record adoptions, and keep a roster of fosters with every cat they have cared for.
  • Edit the home page headline and intro herself.

The two kittens listed on the site right now, Tony and Rony, are my own foster kittens from Brittany. So when I tested the adoption pages, I was writing about two little troublemakers who were wrestling on my couch at the time, and I wanted their pages to do them justice.

Why Next.js

The site had to be fast. Most adopters will open it from a Facebook or Instagram link on their phone, maybe on patchy mobile data, and a slow page is a page people close. Next.js let me build it so that:

  • Pages are ready before anyone asks for them. The public pages are rendered ahead of time and cached close to the visitor, so they load almost instantly instead of being built on every visit.
  • They stay fresh anyway. Cached pages refresh every hour, and right away when Brittany edits a cat, so a cat who has just been adopted disappears from the list without her doing anything extra.
  • Phones download very little. Most of the work happens on the server, so the browser gets plain HTML and only a small amount of JavaScript.
  • Google can read it. Every page has proper titles, descriptions and link previews, so someone searching for cat adoption in Regina can find her, and shared links look right.
  • One codebase does everything: the public site, the admin, the photo uploads and the emails, which keeps it simple to look after.

The hardest part: where it lives

Building the site was the fun part. The real challenge was finding a home for it. A site like this is not just pages: it needs somewhere to run the backend, a database for the cats and applications, and somewhere for uploaded photos to go. Most hosting for that comes with a monthly bill, and every dollar Brittany spends on hosting is a dollar she cannot spend on food or a vet visit.

The first version ran on Supabase with a Postgres database. It worked well, but I wanted every piece on one free plan with plenty of room to spare. So on launch day I moved the whole backend to Cloudflare, where the free tier covers all of it, and a small rescue will never come close to the limits:

The only thing Brittany pays for is her domain. Everything else costs $0 a month.

Launch day

I started building on October 14, 2026, and bnrescues.com went live on October 27, thirteen days and thirty commits later. Launch day itself was busy: moving the backend to Cloudflare, connecting her domain and email, adding dark mode and bonded pairs, and fixing the last few things you only find on a live site.

I am so proud, as a developer, to be able to make a difference with what I know how to do. I cannot take in thirty cats. But I can make sure the people who want to adopt or foster one of Brittany’s cats can find them in a few taps, and that the rescue never pays a cent for it.
